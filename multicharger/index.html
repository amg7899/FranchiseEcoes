I. What is the ECOES MultiCharger

The ECOES MultiCharger is a complex, independent engineering installation that produces AC/DC electricity for charging electric vehicles and other sites, within the single cloud ecosystem of ECOES Franchise for remote monitoring and support.

The ECOES installation is a MultiCharger, because:

  1. 1

    It can charge all kinds of cars, trucks, buses, tractors and other mobile machines with electric motors with DC electricity.

  2. 2

    It can perform DC fast charging up to 100 kW for one, two or three vehicles (1 x 100 kW, 2 x 50 kW, 3 x 33.3 kW).

  3. 3

    It can charge electric vehicles and other sites in any climate, from −35 to +45 degrees Celsius.

  4. 4

    It works equally well with any infrastructure – in the city, on the highway, in the desert, in the jungle and anywhere else.

  5. 5

    It can be mounted on a truck to move on its own, or be towed on a trailer by a pickup or an SUV with a B+E licence.

  6. 6

    It can work as a mobile or stationary DC charging station for vehicles, producing its own AC electricity or taking AC power from the grid.

  7. 7

    It can produce electricity and supply 230/400 V AC to buildings, machines or other sites during power cuts or in remote places without a power supply.

  8. 8

    The same machine can supply AC at 50 Hz and 60 Hz through a 60/50 Hz converter. The generator can be ordered to meet emission standard EU Stage II/III or EU Stage V.

  9. 9

    It can run continuously or in standby, charging electric vehicles with DC or supplying AC power to buildings, machines or other sites.

  10. 10

    It can take payments by card or QR code on its own and issue receipts and invoices to customers, with no operator or accountant involved.

  11. 11

    It can offer customers everyday services: mobile vending machines for hot and cold drinks and snacks, a WC and a washbasin with hot and cold water.

  12. 12

    On its own or in a network with other chargers, it can be monitored, diagnosed and configured remotely through the single cloud ecosystem of ECOES Franchise.

II. Why the ECOES MultiCharger

Because the future belongs to electric cars, and there will be more and more of them. That means more and more charging stations. Charging stations are built where there is enough electricity – in cities and at certain points of the existing road network. On roads in remote and intercity areas there is not enough electricity for charging stations, and such places far outnumber those that have it. That is why the task of the ECOES MultiCharger mobile charging stations is not to compete with stationary charging stations, but to fill the gap where there are none, and where there never will be without enough electricity.

DC electricity from mobile fast-charging stations sells at a higher price than at stationary charging stations, and that is logical, because power from the grid is much cheaper. Customers see and hear the generator of the ECOES MultiCharger running on expensive diesel, and accept the higher charging price with understanding. Charging in remote areas where there are no other charging stations, the ECOES MultiCharger does not really sell electricity: it sells the security and peace of mind that the cars will reach their destination.

A great advantage of the ECOES MultiCharger is its mobility. The franchisee watches the traffic and positions the charging stations in different places, wherever they will bring the most revenue.

The customer service zone brings additional revenue. A car charges for about 30 minutes on average. Meanwhile the driver and the waiting passengers are sure customers for the WC and for water, coffee, packaged snacks, energy drinks and more. Other petrol and diesel cars that do not need charging will also stop for the WC, coffee, water and other products. In this way the customer service zone generates about 20% additional revenue on top of the revenue from charging.

III. Construction and mobility

Construction

The ECOES MultiCharger is housed in a specially designed and certified mobile container, divided into three sections:

Power section – includes a diesel generator AC 400 V 50/60 Hz, 100 kW, 3 DC fast chargers 400 V – 800 V (1 x 100 kW, 2 x 50 kW, 3 x 33.3 kW), DC energy meters (MID), cloud billing accepting POS payments by debit/credit card and QR code, an invoicing system, and hardware for remote monitoring, configuration and service of the charging station.

Customer service section – includes vending machines for coffee, tea and other hot drinks, a vending machine for packaged food and cold drinks – water, soft drinks and energy drinks, folding tables, chairs and umbrellas, and a WC with a washbasin with hot and cold water. Cloud billing accepting POS payments by debit/credit card and QR code for the products consumed. A LiFePO4 battery system with an inverter powers the vending machines.

Operator’s section – includes a separate room with a bed, a desk with a chair, a locker for personal belongings, storage cabinets for vending products and a Wi-Fi connection. The operator does not handle money. All payments for charging and for customer services go through the payment system of ECOES Franchise, through which the money goes straight into the franchisee’s bank account.

The operator’s task is to assist customers while their cars charge, to protect the charging station against damage to the vending machines and theft of products, to keep it clean after use, and to keep in touch with the franchisee and the dispatch centre of ECOES Franchise when service support is needed.

The inner and outer walls of the charging station’s container are made of three layers of thermal and sound insulation, 55 mm thick. LED lighting illuminates the area around the charging station. The vending machines and the DC chargers are protected by lockable waterproof shutters when the charging station is not in operation.

Mobility

The ECOES MultiCharger is a mobile installation that travels on a trailer with a gross weight of up to 3,500 kg. The main unit of the ECOES MultiCharger is the same whichever vehicle it is placed on.

Or on a truck with a gross weight of at least 7,000 kg.

A new or used truck for mounting the ECOES MultiCharger is supplied by the customer.

Mounting on a trailer has the following advantages:

  • A much lower price, because no truck is bought
  • The towing vehicle (a pickup, an SUV or another car) with a weight of up to 3,500 kg is driven with a category B licence.
  • One towing car can move several ECOES MultiCharger units and do other jobs the rest of the time, while a truck stands idle.
  • The trailer is bought as a ready, registered product, while a truck can only be registered after type approval.

Towing a trailer with a gross weight of 3,500 kg requires a category E licence, whether the towing vehicle is a pickup or a truck.

Road signs

Road signs matter, because cars will drive past the ECOES MultiCharger without realising that it offers what they need. That is why signs visible from afar must be placed at a suitable distance of 50–100 m in front of and behind the charging station, so that drivers notice the charging station and slow down to stop.

1. Operating profitability in mobile mode

Stationary DC fast-charging stations powered from the grid sell electricity for charging electric cars at a price 2 – 2.5 times (200% – 250%) higher than the price at which they buy it from the energy suppliers. In this pricing the main factor is the price at which the electricity is bought. With the ECOES MultiCharger the price of the electricity produced depends on the price of the diesel that runs the diesel generator. The diesel consumption for producing 1 kWh of DC electricity, after deducting all mechanical and electrical losses, is 0.290 L/kWh (we take 0.3 L/kWh).

The price of diesel differs substantially between continents and countries. The average price of diesel in the fourth quarter of 2026 is 2.0 EUR/L incl. VAT in Europe, 1.66 USD/L (1.48 EUR/L) in North America, 1.50 USD/L (1.34 EUR/L) in Africa, 1.30 USD/L (1.16 EUR/L) in Latin America, and so on. Such large differences make it impossible to calculate a profitability valid for all countries. That is why we determine the profitability on the basis of diesel prices in Europe; for the other continents and countries the profitability will be proportional to the one calculated for Europe.

Since diesel for the generator accounts for about 90% – 95% of all costs, we determine the profitability from the fuel costs and the gross profit from selling DC fast-charging electricity. Revenue from serving customers at the vending machines also carries a mark-up of 2 – 2.5 times. We forecast the profitability of this activity in the range of 50 €/day to 200 €/day, depending on the commercial load (50 EUR/day at 25%, 100 EUR/day at 50%, 150 EUR/day at 75% and 200 EUR/day at 100% load).

With a consumption of 0.3 L/kWh for producing 1 kWh of DC fast-charging electricity and a diesel price of 2.0 EUR/L, the cost is 0.60 EUR/kWh (0.3 L/kWh x 2.0 EUR/L = 0.6 EUR/kWh). Applying the current rule that the selling price of DC fast-charging electricity is 2 – 2.5 times higher than the price at which the electricity is acquired, the selling price of electricity for cars comes to 1.20 EUR/kWh – 1.50 EUR/kWh. We do the calculations with the lower selling price of 1.20 EUR/kWh. At this price the gross profit from selling 1 kWh of DC fast-charging electricity is 0.60 EUR/kWh (1.20 EUR/kWh selling price – 0.60 EUR/kWh production cost = 0.60 EUR/kWh gross profit).

The price of 1.20 EUR/kWh for DC fast charging in Europe, when needed, is perfectly normal. It is not a price for everyday charging. It is a price for emergency charging from a diesel generator, when the car travels through remote areas where there is no power supply and no stationary charging stations.

2. Operating profitability in stationary mode

The ECOES MultiCharger can work in stationary mode wherever there is enough free AC electricity, 400 V, 100 kW. Such places are roadside restaurants, motels, petrol stations, car parks and others. Stationary operation has some advantages and disadvantages.

The advantages are that the diesel generator does not wear, there is no need to move from place to place, and a lower charging price can be offered while keeping the same gross profit. The disadvantages are that, without mobility, the ECOES MultiCharger cannot be positioned where the traffic is heaviest or in the remote places where a charging station is needed most. The profit from serving customers is lost, because a position next to an existing roadside business will take the customers away. And the franchisee depends on another party, who has to provide the electricity for the charging station in stationary mode.

Being able to work both mobile and stationary allows different modes or a mixed mode – for example, working in mobile mode in remote places during the day, and in stationary mode next to a roadside or city business in the evening.

The average price of electricity in Europe is about 0.30 EUR/kWh incl. VAT. This shows that electricity in stationary mode is half the price of electricity from the diesel generator (0.60 EUR/kWh). This difference makes it possible to lower the price for customers and keep the same gross profit, by setting the selling price at 3 times the price at which the electricity is bought. In this case the selling price of DC fast-charging electricity will be 0.90 EUR/kWh, keeping the gross profit at 0.60 EUR/kWh (0.90 EUR/kWh selling price – 0.30 EUR/kWh purchase price = 0.60 EUR/kWh gross profit).

The price of 0.90 EUR/kWh for DC fast charging in Europe, when needed, is perfectly normal. It is not a price for everyday charging. It is a price for emergency charging, when the car travels through remote areas where there are no stationary charging stations.
